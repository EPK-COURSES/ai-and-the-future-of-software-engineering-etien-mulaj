This research is a huge reality check. It turns out AI isn't as perfect as everyone thinks. The most surprising part is that even though AI writes code super fast, it actually makes a lot of extra work. It copies random stuff from the internet, so it ends up making super messy code with hidden bugs. Senior devs are spending way too much time fixing AI mistakes instead of just doing their actual work.
The stuff about junior developers is definitely the scariest part. New grads usually start with easy coding tasks to learn the ropes, but since AI can do all that basic stuff now, it feels like companies won't want to hire beginners anymore. How is anyone supposed to get experience if there are no entry-level jobs left? Plus, there's a big worry that people will get lazy, just copy-paste everything, and never actually learn how to think for themselves.
But there are some cool parts to it too. If AI does all the boring, repetitive coding and fixes basic typos, developers can focus on the fun stuff like problem-solving and designing cool features. You can also use AI to build your own apps and side projects way faster than before.
Honestly, this totally changes how I look at the future of tech. It's not about AI stealing everyone's jobs, it's just changing what the job is. Just knowing how to write code isn't enough anymore. Future engineers have to be good at the big-picture stuff, double-checking the AI, and actually talking to people. It's definitely a little scary, but it's also pretty exciting.
